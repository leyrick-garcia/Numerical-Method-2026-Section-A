Residual Pattern Interpretation

  • Centering: mean residual is 0.0000 m (essentially zero overall), but the scatter is not locally centered — it traces a smooth, repeating wave rather than random noise around the line.
  • Runs: the longest unbroken run of same-sign residuals is 54 consecutive points (13.5 h) — far longer than the 1–3 points expected from a correctly specified model.
  • Spread vs. level: residual std. dev. is 0.088 m (low third), 0.080 m (mid third), 0.060 m (high third) — the spread does not widen with stage; error is tied to curve-bending regions, not water level.
  • Largest error: 0.221 m at t = 30.25 h, about 22× the logger's 0.01 m resolution — a real structural mismatch, not sensor noise.
  • Conclusion: the residual pattern indicates a failed fit for local shape despite the high R²; R² alone is not sufficient evidence of a good fit for this record.

Units are meter–hours (m·h) — a time-integral of stage, not a volume.

The two areas differ by only 0.03 m·h (-0.002% of the trapezoidal value) because quad integrates the smooth fitted curve while trapezoid integrates the raw, centimeter-rounded readings, so the difference is just the net effect of the fitting residuals cancelling out over the record.

For a flood-control office: this m·h value is a compact summary of how high and how long the reservoir stayed elevated above baseline during the event — useful for ranking or comparing event severity/duration on a common scale. It is not a water volume and must not be used directly to size spillway capacity or compute inflow/outflow volumes; that requires multiplying by the reservoir's stage-dependent storage area, which is not part of this dataset.
